I work in the University of Virginia's Dependable Systems and Analytics group in the Link Lab. My research combines synchronized sensing and multimodal machine learning to understand human activity and support safer healthcare workflows.

Current Research

EgoEMS and CognitiveEMS

I led the collection and release of EgoEMS, an egocentric EMS dataset with more than 20 hours of data from 233 simulated emergency scenarios and 62 participants, including 46 EMS professionals. It supports research on real-time keystep recognition and action quality estimation. The related CognitiveEMS system uses wearable sensing and augmented reality to provide context-aware assistance to responders. Read the EgoEMS paper and CognitiveEMS paper.

MiDAS and Surgical Activity Prediction

I co-developed MiDAS, a platform-agnostic system for collecting synchronized surgical video and hand and foot tracking data. It was validated on Raven-II and da Vinci Xi robot-assisted surgery platforms. My related work uses multimodal Transformers for real-time surgical activity prediction and safety monitoring. Read the MiDAS paper and surgical activity prediction paper.
